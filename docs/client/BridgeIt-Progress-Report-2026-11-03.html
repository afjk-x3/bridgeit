BridgeIt v2 · 3 November 2026

BridgeIt progress report

All three levels of the new BridgeIt are playable from start to finish. This week we reviewed every screen against the design and changed anything that was hard to read, hard to use or unclear for a Grade 8 student.

ReadyAll three levels, the unlock codes, the PDF of answers for the teacher, and the art.
Waiting for youThe Level 2 sentences and the song, due 7 November.
Delivery21 November 2026.

What changed this week

Easier to read and use

  • Text is now at least 14 px everywhere, including the song timer and the character counters.
  • Greyed-out buttons keep a dark, readable label.
  • Each screen's header says where the student is, for example "LEVEL 2 · BRIDGE 3 OF 7".
  • The app keeps still for students who turn off animations or use a screen reader.

Level 1: Conjunction Theme Song

  • The song player now sits at the top of the lyrics card, so the words and the player stay together.
  • The MatchIt instructions show an example: a finger dragging "to add" to the box beside "and".
  • All seven MatchIt rows fit on the screen, and the board stays the same size while the student matches functions.

Level 2: Building Bridges

  • One bridge everywhere. The Start screen, the Welcome screen and every Level 2 step show the same suspension bridge. The two clauses are written on the bridge deck on either side of the gap.
  • Wrong answers. A wrong word stays in the gap, outlined in orange, with a hint that says what that word does (for example, "'nor' connects negative alternatives"). The first wrong try cracks the left support of the bridge and the second cracks the right one. The third collapses the bridge, the car falls into the river, and the bridge restarts. The counter reads "2 tries left", then "1 try left".
  • Strengthening the bridge. Each sentence the student writes becomes a support pier under the bridge, numbered and labelled with the word they used ("1 · for", "2 · because"). Their sentences are listed in full under the bridge. Until the piers are in, the bridge gently shakes; once they're all in, it stands still.
  • Quicker writing. After the first sentence, the student goes straight to writing with the Quick Facts words. The extra "tap the conjunction" step and the Quick Facts pop-up are gone; the Quick Facts card sits above the bridge instead.
  • Help while writing. A checklist under the writing box ticks off as the student types: starts with a capital, uses the conjunction, comma right before it, ends with . ! or ?. After a first wrong attempt, a hint explains what the conjunction does and shows an example sentence.

Level 3: Travelogue

  • The map. It now runs north to south along a winding road: Saud Beach, Bangui, Burgos, Laoag La Paz, Paoay, Currimao and Badoc. Each place has a numbered stop and a picture card, and all seven show without scrolling. The student picks a place, then taps "Drive to …".
  • Place photos. The screen shows the place's name, one dot for each photo, and a "Map" button to go back and choose again.
  • Writing the travelogue. When the keyboard opens, the writing box keeps its room. Until the entry is ready, the Submit button says what is missing, for example "Write 2 more sentences to submit" or "Use 1 more conjunction to submit".
  • The last screen. It keeps its road and car, and its main button now reads "Send my answers to my teacher".
